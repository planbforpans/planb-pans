Constipation in kids that won’t go away.
When to look deeper.

Most constipation in children is ordinary, and it gets better with ordinary fixes. But when it doesn’t, or when it comes with sudden changes in mood or behavior, it can be a clue worth following rather than something to manage forever.

Call the doctor promptly if you see

Blood in the stool, a swollen or very painful belly, vomiting, weight loss or poor growth, fever, constipation since birth, or weakness or numbness in the legs.

Start with the usual causes

Most childhood constipation is functional: too little fiber or fluid, busy days, and holding stools in because going has hurt before, which becomes a cycle. A regular toilet routine, fluids, fiber and the stool softener your doctor suggests resolve most cases.

Signs it may be more

It got better on antibiotics

If constipation suddenly improved during a course of antibiotics, then came right back when it ended, read that as a clue, not a cure. Something the antibiotic hit is still there, and repeated courses can keep resetting the gut without fixing the cause.

It arrived with behavior changes

Constipation that started around the same time as sudden OCD, rage, anxiety, food refusal or new bedwetting is worth mentioning to your doctor alongside PANS and PANDAS.

What to ask your doctor about

1 · Perianal strep

Strep around the bottom makes stools painful, so children hold them in. Throat swabs miss it. Ask for a perianal strep swab for your child and anyone at home with redness or itching, and a re-swab 2–4 weeks after treatment.

2 · Methane overgrowth

Methane-producing microbes slow the gut down. A lactulose breath test that measures methane and hydrogen checks for it.

3 · Clostridia or C. difficile

Markers on an organic acids test and a C. diff toxin test on stool, especially after many antibiotic courses.

4 · Bile acids and fat handling

Antibiotics change how bile acids reach the colon, where they act as a natural laxative. A pattern like that points to how fat is being handled. See the gut and the microbiome.
