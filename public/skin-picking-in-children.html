Skin picking in kids.
When it’s more than a habit.

Picking at skin, scabs or lips is common in children, and usually it’s a way of handling anxiety or a restless urge. But when it starts suddenly, alongside other changes, it can be one piece of a bigger picture.

Usually: a habit worth gentle help

Skin picking belongs to a group called body-focused repetitive behaviors, alongside nail biting and hair pulling. Shame and punishment tend to make it worse. Habit-reversal training with a therapist, keeping hands busy, and treating any anxiety underneath help most children.

See a doctor if

Sores look infected (spreading redness, warmth, pus, or honey-colored crusts), picking causes bleeding or scarring, or it arrived together with other sudden changes.

When it started suddenly

If picking appeared over days, together with new OCD, anxiety, rage, food refusal, tics, sleep problems or bedwetting, the pattern matters more than any one symptom. A sudden start of several symptoms together is the hallmark of PANS and PANDAS, which can follow strep or another infection.

What to bring to the doctor

When each symptom started, any illness in the weeks before, and whether anyone at home has had strep. Our printable symptoms checklist makes that easy.
